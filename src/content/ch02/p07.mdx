<Box tone="a" span step={19}>

Real rain does not come in three neat sizes. It comes as a continuous **drop size distribution**, written `N(D)` — how many drops per cubic metre, per millimetre of diameter, at each size.

Our sums become integrals, and that is the only thing that changes:

<Calc>{`Z = ∫ D^{6} N(D) dD       R = 6π×10^{−4} ∫ D^{3} v(D) N(D) dD
[[cm:   …same two weightings. Same ambiguity. Just smoother.]]`}</Calc>

</Box>

<Box step={20}>

GPM models `N(D)` as a **normalised gamma distribution**:

<Calc>{`N(D) = [[s:N_{w}]] f(μ) (D/[[s:D_{m}]])^{μ} e^{−(4+μ)D/[[s:D_{m}]]}`}</Calc>

Look at what is <span className="sv">blue</span>. The two unknowns from the last page are the two parameters of the curve. Everything else is assumption.

</Box>

<Box tone="d" step={21}>

And **μ**, the shape factor? The algorithm *assumes* it rather than retrieving it — there is not enough information to do otherwise.

<Talk char="dot2">So you've got one measurement, two unknowns, and a third parameter you're just… guessing?</Talk>
