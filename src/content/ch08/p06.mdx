<Box tone="a" span step={14}>

A Level-2 granule is one orbit, in HDF5. Everything in this book is a few lines away:

<Calc code>{`[[cm:import h5py, numpy as np]]

f  = h5py.File(path, [[o:"r"]])
fs = f[[[o:"FS"]]]                               [[cm:# the full-swath group]]

lat   = fs[[[o:"Latitude"]]][:]                   [[cm:# (nscan, 49)]]
lon   = fs[[[o:"Longitude"]]][:]
rate  = fs[[[o:"SLV/precipRate"]]][:]             [[cm:# (nscan, 49, 176)]]
hgt   = fs[[[o:"PRE/height"]]][:]                 [[cm:# (nscan, 49, 176), metres]]
ptype = fs[[[o:"CSF/typePrecip"]]][:]             [[cm:# (nscan, 49)]]
cfb   = fs[[[o:"PRE/binClutterFreeBottom"]]][:]   [[cm:# (nscan, 49), an index]]`}</Calc>

</Box>

<Box step={15}>

Note `PRE/height`. The file **gives you the height of every range bin**, already corrected for the slant path and the local zenith angle.

<Psst>Do not compute it yourself from bin numbers and 125 m. People do, and they get the swath edge wrong.</Psst>

</Box>

<Box tone="d" step={16}>

Before anything else, **print the shapes**. Some fields in the dual-frequency product carry a trailing frequency axis of length 2 — Ku first, then Ka — and some do not:

<Calc code>{`for k in [[[o:"PRE/zFactorMeasured"]],
          [[o:"SLV/zFactorFinal"]],
          [[o:"SLV/paramDSD"]]]:
    print(k, fs[k].shape)`}</Calc>

It costs one second and saves an afternoon.

</Box>
