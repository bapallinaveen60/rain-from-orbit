<Box tone="a" step={11}>

Validation over India leans on three independent sources:

- **IMD gridded gauge analysis** — dense over land, the usual reference for monsoon-season evaluation.
- **Ground radar and disdrometers** — these give the *actual* drop size distribution rather than an inference of it.
- **Inter-comparison** between DPR, IMERG and the earlier TMPA products over the monsoon core region.

</Box>

<Box tone="d" step={12}>

The consistent pattern in that literature: satellite estimates capture the **spatial structure and seasonal cycle** well, and struggle most with **heavy orographic events** and with **light rain**.

<Psst>Two tails, two different reasons — and you now know both of them.</Psst>

</Box>

<Box tone="c" span step={13}>

<Alert title="IF YOU WORK WITH THIS DATA!!!">

<b>Stratify before you compare anything.</b> A bulk bias statistic over a whole season mixes wet-spell convection, break-spell shallow rain and stratiform decks — three regimes the algorithm handled with three different sets of assumptions.

<p style={{ marginBottom: 0 }}>The interesting signal is almost always in the stratification, not in the mean.</p>

</Alert>
