The Solver works on a two-dimensional plane: **rain rate** up the vertical axis, **mass-weighted mean diameter** across the bottom. Every possible answer for a range bin is a point on it.

Two things are drawn on that plane, and the answer is where they cross.

</Box>

<Box tone="plain">

<figure>

<InlineSvg svg={fig1} />

<figcaption>One crossing is the happy case. The next step is what happens when there are two — or none.</figcaption>

</figure>

</Box>

<Box tone="d" span step={9}>

The two curves:

- <b>The <span className="sv">R–D<sub>m</sub> relation</span></b> — an assumption, of the form <code>R = ε<sup>r</sup> p D<sub>m</sub><sup>q</sup></code>, chosen by the DSD module from the rain type.
- <b>The <span className="ov">Z<sub>f</sub> contour</span></b> — every (R, D<sub>m</sub>) pair that would produce the reflectivity actually observed at this bin, drawn from the Mie scattering tables.
