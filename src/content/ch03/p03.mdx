<Header>Three Heights That Matter: Details Part 2</Header>

<Grid>

<Box tone="a" step={7}>

<b>`binStormTop`</b> — the highest bin with a detected precipitation echo. Above it, nothing.

It matters more than it looks: the forward retrieval *starts* here, so an error in storm top propagates down the whole profile as an attenuation error.

</Box>

<Box step={8}>

<b>`binRealSurface`</b> — the ground, found from the echo power profile together with the digital elevation model carried in the Level-1B file.

</Box>

<Box tone="c" span step={9}>

<b>`binClutterFreeBottom`</b> — the lowest bin Core is willing to trust. And it sits well *above* the surface.

The surface return leaks into nearby bins through the antenna's main lobe and, worse, through its side lobes. The effect gets dramatically worse away from nadir, and here is why:

</Box>

<Alert title="THE CONSEQUENCE!!!">

Everything below the clutter-free bottom is **extrapolated, not measured**. The Solver simply holds reflectivity constant from the lowest trusted bin down to the ground.

<p style={{ marginBottom: 0 }}>For deep convection that is defensible. For shallow rain over mountains it is a guess about the exact part of the profile you cared about.</p>

</Alert>
