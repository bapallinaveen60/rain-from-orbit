<Opener kicker="Chapter 03" title="Slicing the Storm!!!">

Range is height. That one fact is what makes a radar worth putting in orbit — and it comes with a blind spot right where you least want one.

</Opener>

<Header kind="blue">The Scan Curtain: Main Ideas</Header>

<Grid>

<Box tone="a" step={1}>

<b>The idea:</b> Core sweeps its antenna across the track, **49 footprints** per sweep, each about 5.5 km wide at nadir. The spacecraft's own motion advances the scan forward.

Stack enough sweeps and you have a three-dimensional volume of the storm.

</Box>

<Box step={2}>

Within each beam, the echo is chopped into **range bins** — 125 m apart, sampled from 19 km above the ellipsoid all the way down to the ground.

Most of those bins are empty air. The interesting ones are the handful that are not.

</Box>

<Box tone="d" span step={3}>

<Talk char="core">There is one problem, **Dot**, and it is at the bottom. The ground is by far the brightest thing in the scene — orders of magnitude brighter than you.</Talk>

<Talk char="dot2">So the bottom of your curtain is ruined by the echo from the dirt?</Talk>

<Talk char="core2">Several hundred metres of it. More near the edge of the swath. And that is the layer where the rain actually reaches the ground.</Talk>

<Psst>That layer has a name — the **clutter blind layer** — and Chapter 8 will show you why it is a serious problem over the Western Ghats.</Psst>

</Box>
