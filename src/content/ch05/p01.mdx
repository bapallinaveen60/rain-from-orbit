One measurement, two unknowns. You cannot assume your way out of that. You need a second measurement — at a wavelength where the drops stop behaving.

</Opener>

<Header kind="blue">Dual Frequency: Main Ideas</Header>

<Grid>

<Box tone="a" step={1}>

<Talk char="core">If my second radar behaved *exactly* like my first, the second measurement would be redundant and we would have gained nothing.</Talk>

<Talk char="dot">It doesn't behave the same, though. At 8.4 millimetres I'm not a small object any more.</Talk>

</Box>

<Box step={2}>

<b>The idea in one line:</b> because the two bands disagree, and because *how much* they disagree depends on drop size, the **difference between them** measures size directly.

<Calc>{`[[b:DFR]] = 10 log_{10} Z([[kut:Ku]]) − 10 log_{10} Z([[kat:Ka]])`}</Calc>

Dual-Frequency Ratio. A difference of logs, so really a ratio.

</Box>

<Box tone="d" span step={3}>

**And here is the elegant part**, which we will prove properly in two pages: in the ratio, the *concentration cancels out*.

<Calc>{`DFR tells you [[s:D_{m}]]   (and nothing about [[s:N_{w}]])
then either reflectivity tells you [[s:N_{w}]]`}</Calc>

Two measurements, two unknowns, system closed.
