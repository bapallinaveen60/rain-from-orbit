<Box tone="a" step={13}>

At each range bin we now have two equations and two unknowns — plus an attenuation term that has to come from somewhere. There are two places to start.

</Box>

<Box step={14}>

**FORWARD** — start at storm top, work down.

The attenuation above any bin is known, because you already solved the bins above it.

<i>The cost:</i> it inherits the Hitschfeld–Bordan instability from Chapter 4, **and** the double-valued trough from the last page — at every single bin.

</Box>

<Box tone="d" step={15}>

**BACKWARD** — start at the surface, work up.

Now the *known* quantity is the attenuation below each bin, anchored by the SRT's independent PIA estimate.

<i>The cost:</i> you depend on the SRT being right, and on an assumption about the profile inside the clutter blind layer that nobody can check.

</Box>

<Box tone="c" span step={16}>

The ATBD is unambiguous about which one wins: the backward method is preferred because it generally gives **numerically stable and unique** solutions, where the forward method may have two solutions at each range bin.

<Talk char="core">In practice I do not commit to either. Chapter 7 shows what I actually do — I run the retrieval many times over and pick the version that best reconciles everything I know.</Talk>

</Box>
