The dielectric constant of the mixture comes from a **mixing rule** — not an average, but one weighted the way scattering actually responds:

<Calc>{`(ε_{b} − 1)/(ε_{b} + U) = P_{w}(ε_{w} − 1)/(ε_{w} + U) + P_{i}(ε_{i} − 1)/(ε_{i} + U)

U = 12.0                          if ρ_{b} ≤ 0.09
U = 2.0 · e^{13(ρ_{b} − 0.09)}            if ρ_{b} > 0.09`}</Calc>

<Note><b>A quirk worth knowing.</b> Evaluate U on both sides of the boundary: at ρ<sub>b</sub> = 0.09 it is **12.0**, and a hair above it is **2.0**. The published formula *steps* there — two different regimes for fluffy aggregates and for denser particles, joined without a smooth transition. It is in the document as printed; if your own forward model disagrees with GPM at very low densities, look here first.</Note>
