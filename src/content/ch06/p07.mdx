**THE V-METHOD** — vertical. Looks down a single beam.

<Calc>{`bright band found  AND Z < 46 dBZ → [[b:stratiform]]
no bright band   AND Z > 40 dBZ → [[b:convective]]
neither                      → [[b:other]]`}</Calc>

<Psst>Two different thresholds, because a bright band is itself strong evidence — it earns a higher bar before the profile is called convective.</Psst>

</Box>

<Box step={15}>

**THE H-METHOD** — horizontal. Ignores the vertical structure entirely and looks at the **pattern across neighbouring pixels**, using a modified Steiner–Houze–Yuter separation.

A pixel much brighter than its surrounding background is convective, whatever its own profile looks like.

This catches the embedded convective cores inside an otherwise stratiform deck, which the V-method would smooth over.

</Box>

<Talk char="dot2">Why does the method use the *slope* of DFR<sub>m</sub> rather than how big it is? Surely the actual value is more informative.</Talk>

<Talk char="core">Because any fixed calibration offset between my two radars adds the *same constant* at every height. Differentiating with height removes it completely. Slope is free of a bias I cannot otherwise measure.</Talk>
