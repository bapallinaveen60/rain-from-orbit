Before Core can choose a drop size distribution, it has to decide what it is looking at. The evidence is the shape of the profile itself.

</Opener>

<Header kind="blue">Classification: Main Ideas</Header>

<Grid>

<Box tone="a" step={1}>

<b>Why bother?</b> Because the microphysics genuinely differs, and the algorithm carries **separate R–D<sub>m</sub> relations** for the two regimes:

<Calc>{`stratiform:  R = 0.401 ε^{4.649} D_{m}^{6.131}
convective:  R = 1.370 ε^{4.258} D_{m}^{5.420}`}</Calc>

Classify a profile wrong and you have applied the wrong physics to the entire column.

</Box>

<Box step={2}>

**Three categories**, and the third one is the honest one:

- **Stratiform** — widespread, gentle, layered.
- **Convective** — vigorous, deep, lumpy.
- **Other** — for echoes that are only cloud, or only noise, or that live entirely above the freezing level where the question has no good answer.

</Box>
