All of this has to be written down per range bin, and the DSD module does it by placing **five nodes** through the profile:

<div className="scrollx">

<table className="t">
<thead>
  <tr><th>Node</th><th>Where</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
  <tr><td className="n">A</td><td>the bin nearest <b>−20 °C</b> — at and above it, Solid</td></tr>
  <tr><td className="n">B</td><td>bright band **top**</td></tr>
  <tr><td className="n">C</td><td>bright band **peak**</td></tr>
  <tr><td className="n">D</td><td>bright band **bottom** — at and below it, Liquid</td></tr>
  <tr><td className="n">E</td><td>the bin nearest <b>+20 °C</b></td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</div>

Between B and D the particle temperature is pinned to **0 °C** whatever the air is doing. Between A and D the phase is Mixed.

</Box>

<Box tone="d" step={22}>

When there is **no bright band** — convective, or stratiform without one — B, C and D all collapse onto the single 0 °C bin.

<Psst>That is the whole difference in code: a melting layer with thickness, or a melting layer with none.</Psst>

<Talk char="core">And it is why Chapter 7 insists the classifier runs *before* the microphysics. Those five bins are the output of CSF and the input to everything that follows.</Talk>

</Box>

<Box tone="c" span step={23}>

The per-bin result is stored in `DSD/phase`, and its encoding is one of the neatest tricks in the product — a **single integer carrying both phase and temperature**:

<div className="scrollx">

<table className="t">
<thead>
  <tr><th>Value</th><th>Means</th><th>Example</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
  <tr><td className="n">≥ 200</td><td>**Liquid**, temperature = value − 200 °C</td><td className="n">210 → liquid at +10 °C</td></tr>
  <tr><td className="n">100–200</td><td>**In the bright band**: 100 = top, 150 = peak, 200 = bottom</td><td className="n">125 and 175 are the only intermediates used</td></tr>
  <tr><td className="n">\< 100</td><td>**Frozen or part-frozen**, temperature = value − 100 °C</td><td className="n">80 → ice at −20 °C</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</div>

<Alert title="READ THIS BEFORE YOU PLOT IT!!!">

<p style={{ marginBottom: 0 }}>`phase` is **not** a continuous field. Plotting it on a colour scale produces something that looks meaningful and is not — a jump from 99 to 200 is a change of 1 °C across the melting boundary, not a change of 101. Decode it into phase and temperature first, then plot those separately.</p>

</Alert>
